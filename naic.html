About the Programme

Moving N-ATLAS from model availability to national infrastructure

The National AI Innovation Challenge is designed to deepen the technical development, adoption and practical use of Nigeria’s sovereign AI capabilities.

At the centre is N-ATLAS (Nigerian Atlas for Languages and AI at Scale), Nigeria’s first open-source, multilingual large language model. Fine-tuned from Llama-3 8B, it supports Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Nigerian-accented English, with Automatic Speech Recognition for voice applications.

The challenge directs Nigerian talent towards building developer tools, voice-first applications, and sector-specific AI solutions on N-ATLAS.

Learn more about N-ATLAS

N-ATLAS Capabilities

  • Multilingual LLM supporting Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, Nigerian English
  • Automatic Speech Recognition for voice-driven applications
  • Open-source model fine-tuned from Llama-3 8B

Why This Challenge

From open-source model to national infrastructure

Making an AI model available is a first step. Turning it into infrastructure that developers, businesses and citizens can use requires tooling, applications and specialised adaptations.

01

Make N-ATLAS easier to build with

Develop infrastructure, documentation and tools that enable developers to integrate and extend N-ATLAS efficiently.

02

Put N-ATLAS into the hands of Nigerians

Build voice-first services that allow more Nigerians to access digital services through supported languages and accessible channels.

03

Adapt N-ATLAS to high-impact sectors

Develop specialised versions that understand the terminology and contexts of sectors where AI can deliver meaningful impact.

Programme Objectives

What the challenge aims to achieve

  1. 01Deepen the technical development of N-ATLAS through working developer tools, fine-tuned models, datasets and ecosystem contributions.
  2. 02Accelerate adoption through deployable, real-world applications, particularly voice-first services.
  3. 03Expand use across high-impact sectors including health, agriculture, education, legal services and financial inclusion.
  4. 04Connect Nigeria’s universities, private sector and government through a structured innovation ecosystem.
  5. 05Cultivate and showcase Nigerian AI talent nationally and internationally.

01 Developer Infrastructure

Build the tools that make N-ATLAS easy to build with

N-ATLAS is open-source and downloadable, but Nigerian-maintained tooling is needed to help developers move from accessing the model to integrating, testing, adapting and deploying it.

Example Solutions

  • Python or JavaScript SDK with clean abstractions over N-ATLAS endpoints
  • Interactive browser-based playground for testing N-ATLAS outputs
  • Fine-tuning starter kit with training scripts and evaluation tools
  • Bilingual developer documentation with working examples

Who should apply

Teams with expertise in developer tooling, model integration, documentation or AI engineering.

Key Requirement

The solution must directly integrate with N-ATLAS. Wrapping another general-purpose model does not qualify.

02 Voice-First Access

Build applications for Nigerians who do not type

N-ATLAS includes ASR capabilities for Nigerian languages. The challenge is to translate those into useful, accessible services for everyday Nigerians.

Delivery Channels

  • WhatsApp voice note services
  • USSD-based voice applications
  • Interactive Voice Response systems
  • Low-bandwidth mobile applications

Use Cases

  • Education and learning support
  • Agricultural advisory
  • Primary health information
  • Civic information
  • Financial literacy

Who should apply

Teams capable of building accessible digital services and testing with real target users.

Key Requirement

Voice input must use the official N-ATLAS ASR service for the relevant language.

03 Sectoral Fine-Tuning

Fine-tune N-ATLAS for high-impact Nigerian sectors

General-purpose models become more useful when adapted to the terminology, data and context of a particular field.

Target Domains

  • Education and learning
  • Primary healthcare and community health
  • Smallholder agriculture
  • Legal rights and civic literacy
  • Basic financial literacy

Requirements

  • Fine-tune N-ATLAS for one clearly defined domain
  • Demonstrate measurable improvement over base N-ATLAS
  • Deploy through a documented API another developer can integrate

Who should apply

Teams with experience in model fine-tuning, dataset development, benchmarking and evaluation.

Key Requirement

The model must be derived directly from N-ATLAS, not from another foundation model.

Build Requirements

Build, don't just propose

This is a build-only challenge. Every valid submission must demonstrate three things:

01

A working technical build

Software, deployed system, dataset, model or another functioning technical artefact relevant to your problem statement.

02

Genuine N-ATLAS integration

The project must use the N-ATLAS model, API, ASR service or training pipeline as required by your problem statement.

03

Real-world validation

Evidence of testing with actual users, real data or live benchmarks. Not simulated or hypothetical results.

Not sufficient for submission

  • Research papers without a functional build
  • Slide presentations or pitch decks
  • Frameworks or methodologies
  • Mock-ups or wireframes
  • Proposals or concept documents

Evaluation

How applications are assessed

Submissions are assessed by a multidisciplinary panel of technical AI experts, sector specialists and representatives of ONDI and NCAIR.

The evaluation focuses heavily on the quality of the working build and the depth of its integration with N-ATLAS. A functioning artefact is mandatory.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is the National AI Innovation Challenge?

A national build-only challenge inviting Nigerian universities, research institutions, startups, technology companies and independent developers to build working tools, applications and fine-tuned models on N-ATLAS, Nigeria’s sovereign large language model.

What is N-ATLAS?

N-ATLAS (Nigerian Atlas for Languages & AI at Scale) is Nigeria’s first open-source, multilingual large language model. Fine-tuned from Llama-3 8B, it supports Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo and Nigerian-accented English, with ASR for voice applications.

Why is this called a "build-only" challenge?

Every submission must ship a working technical artefact integrated with N-ATLAS and tested with real users, data or benchmarks. Research papers, proposals and frameworks without a functional build will not be evaluated.

Who can apply?

Nigerian universities, research institutions, registered startups, technology companies and independent developers. All team members must be Nigerian citizens or registered Nigerian entities.

What is the difference between Track A and Track B?

Track A (Academic & Research) is for university-based teams with 2–5 members including a faculty supervisor. Track B (Private Sector & Innovation) is for startups, companies and independent developers with 1–6 members.

Can I apply to more than one problem statement?

No. Teams may submit to only one problem statement per submission cycle.

Can I wrap a different foundation model (e.g. GPT-4)?

No. Submissions that wrap general-purpose models instead of N-ATLAS will be disqualified. The core requirement is genuine, demonstrable integration with N-ATLAS.

What counts as "real-world validation"?

Evidence of testing with real users, real data, or live benchmarks. PS1 needs 2+ external beta testers, PS2 needs 50+ documented user interactions, PS3 needs a 500+ example benchmark.

What exactly must I submit?

Seven things: working artefact, N-ATLAS integration evidence, real-world validation, technical documentation, 3–5 minute video, team profile, and endorsement/registration evidence.

When is the deadline?

Applications close 12 October 2026 at 11:59 PM (WAT). Late submissions will not be accepted.
